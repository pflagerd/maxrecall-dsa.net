Putting a question directly into your queue, by its slug

How to get a question you choose into your queue now, rather than waiting for it in your backlog or adding it to your backlog first: open it by its slug, then Submit or Skip it. For you or any learner, with the server running. Open this file straight from disk; it needs no server.

Status: written 2026-11-06. Every step was tried in a copy of the VPS in the cloud (its maxrecall user, folders and service settings; no nginx, systemd or sandbox), in a browser, as the owner: the results under Where it lands are from that run.

A feature, not a gap (owner, 2026-11-06): opening any question by its slug, chosen backlog or not, and its Submit or Skip putting it into the queue, are meant to work. Don't make the server refuse a question outside the queue or backlog; the profile's languages are what's enforced. Recorded in readme.html's Decisions, routes.js (checkExecutionRequest) and order.js (recordSubmit), and guarded by a test in order.test.js.

The authorities are readme.html (“Where data lives”: the queue and backlog; “Front end”: ?language=, Submit and Skip) and the code: order.js (recordSubmit, what a Submit or Skip does to the queue), advance.js and introduce.js. This page explains them; where they disagree, they win.

Your queue, and a question's slug

Your queue holds a question in a language: Two Sum in Python and Two Sum in Java are two entries (the code calls each a pair). Two files in your learning folder, users/<your email>/, hold them:

FileWhat's in itHow a question gets there
order.json, your queueWhat you're practising, the next one first. Each has passesInARow, and pass once you've submitted it.A Submit or Skip of it (this page), or from the backlog at a Submit or Skip
backlog.json, your backlogQuestions waiting to come in, in order.make backlog-add (a chosen backlog), or the server (a backlog of "all")

A question's slug is its file name in questions/ without .json: two-sum for questions/two-sum.json. It's in the address of every question page: problem.html?slug=two-sum&language=python.

The key fact: a Submit or Skip of a question that isn't in your queue, whether it's in your backlog or in neither file, puts it straight into the queue (order.js, recordSubmit, rule 1). Run changes nothing. And you can open any question by its slug, even one you haven't chosen: a chosen backlog limits what the list and Next offer, not what can be opened (docs/deploy-vps.md §11).

The steps

  1. Find its slug
  2. Open it by its slug
  3. Submit or Skip it
  4. Check it's in your queue

Then: where it lands (not always first), what it costs, other ways, on Kubuntu and the checklist. The server keeps running throughout.

1. Find its slug

  • From a question page you've had open: the slug= part of the address.
  • Every question, with where each is for you (queue, backlog or not chosen), on the VPS as root. It only reads, so the server can keep running:
cd /srv/maxrecall/maxrecall-dsa.net
sudo -u maxrecall env MAXRECALL_USERS_DIR=/srv/maxrecall/data/users make backlog-show EMAIL=daniel.pflager@gmail.com
  • sudo -u maxrecall: as the service's user, who owns the files (sudo(8)).
  • env MAXRECALL_USERS_DIR=…: the service's users folder, not the checkout's own users/.
  • EMAIL: whose queue and backlog to show; the list of every question after them is the same for anyone. The slug is the first column.

2. Open it by its slug

Put the slug, and the language you want, in the question page's address:

https://maxrecallcode.net/problem.html?slug=two-sum&language=python
  • slug=: the question, from step 1.
  • language=: one of javascript, python, cpp, java, d. It must be one your profile allows, that you've turned on, and that the question has; otherwise an alert says which, and the question opens in your usual language. Left out, the question opens in your default language. It doesn't change your default (readme.html, “Front end”).
  • Through the SSH tunnel (make open-ssh-3005, no sign-in): http://localhost:3005/problem.html?slug=two-sum&language=python.

3. Submit or Skip it

Either one puts it into your queue; they differ in what they record:

ButtonSavesIn the queue it gets
Submit an answerA Submit record, judged against every test casepass "yes" and 1 pass in a row if every case passed; "no" and 0 otherwise
SkipA Skip record: the editor's code, nothing judgedpass "no", 0 passes in a row: a Skip counts as a fail
  • Skip asks first only if you have passes in a row on that question in that language, which a question not yet in your queue doesn't. After a saved Submit the button reads Next and Skip is hidden.
  • Run doesn't put it into the queue: Run records nothing.

4. Check it's in your queue

  • The problem list (https://maxrecallcode.net/) shows the queue first: it's there with ✓ ×1 (passed) or ✗ (failed or skipped).
  • Or make backlog-show as in step 1: it's under “Queue”, and no longer “not chosen” or in the backlog.

Where it lands: in the queue, not always first

A Submit or Skip treats a question from outside the queue as if it had been at the front, then moves it down as any Submit does (order.js, recordSubmit):

  • Moved down advance(passesInARow) places: 1.3 × passes in a row × a random factor from 0.9 to 1.1, rounded at random, at least 1 (advance.js). A Skip or a fail: 1 place, so one question is ahead of it. A first pass: usually 1, sometimes 2. Never further than the end.
  • Then the backlog tops the queue up, at the front: enough to keep at least 3 in the queue, or 1 every 5 Submits, whichever is more (introduce.js). Those come in ahead of it.

To practise it now, you don't need it in the queue. It's open in front of you from step 2: answer it then. To have it be what Next offers first, put it at the front by hand, without a record (below).

What it costs

  • A record, in your Submissions tab and your learning folder: a Submit or a Skip. The app can't take one back.
  • A Skip counts as a fail. On a question new to your queue there are no passes to lose, so it only means its first result is ✗.
  • A Submit's result counts as it is: a pass starts its passes in a row at 1.

Other ways

By hand, with no record, anywhere in the queue

Edit order.json with the server stopped: it rewrites the file at every Submit, so the site is down for everyone for those minutes. On the VPS, as root:

systemctl stop maxrecall-dsa.test.service
SUDO_EDITOR=vi sudoedit -u maxrecall /srv/maxrecall/data/users/daniel.pflager@gmail.com/order.json
  • sudoedit -u maxrecall: edits a copy, then writes it back as maxrecall, so the file stays the service's. Editing it with vi directly as root could leave it root's, and the server couldn't write it any more (sudo(8), -e).
  • SUDO_EDITOR=vi: the editor sudoedit runs.

Add one line to "queue", where you want it; first means it's next:

{"question": "two-sum", "language": "python", "passesInARow": 0},
  • question: the slug. language: as in step 2. passesInARow: 0. No pass: it's added at its first Submit.
  • Mind the commas: JSON allows none after the last element.
  • If it's in backlog.json, take it out there the same way. A question in a language listed in both files, or twice, makes both files invalid, and the server then refuses your Run and Submit until it's fixed (order.js, parseQueueFiles).

Check before starting the server; it only reads:

sudo -u maxrecall env MAXRECALL_USERS_DIR=/srv/maxrecall/data/users make consistency-check
  • “nothing found” for you: done. An error names the problem in the file: fix it and check again. A warning about the question means its slug or language is wrong: it would stay in the queue but never be offered.
systemctl start maxrecall-dsa.test.service

In the test run, the file stayed maxrecall's, the check found nothing, and Next then offered the question added first.

Through your backlog: make backlog-add … AT=1

Puts it at the front of the backlog, with the server stopped (readme.html, “Running it”; how-to-add-a-new-user.html, “More questions”). It moves into the queue only when a Submit or Skip brings backlog questions in, at least 3 in the queue or 1 every 5 Submits (above): not necessarily at once.

Not built: make queue-add

One command that would do the hand edit safely, like make backlog-add does for the backlog. It doesn't exist yet.

On Kubuntu (your own machine)

With make dev running, the same address on your own server:

http://localhost:3000/problem.html?slug=two-sum&language=python

By hand: stop make dev (Ctrl-C), edit users/<your email>/order.json in the checkout with vi (your own files, so no sudoedit), then:

make consistency-check
