## How we work (learned in this session)

- Everything in both handoffs' "How we work" still applies.
- **Fetch one branch at a time when one may not exist yet.**
  `git fetch origin master <new-branch>` fails as a whole when
  `<new-branch>` isn't on the remote yet, and then `origin/master` isn't
  updated either. Comparing against it then compares against an old
  `master` (here it listed some hundred commits as "not in master").
  `git fetch origin master` on its own did it.
- After a merge, `git merge-base --is-ancestor HEAD origin/master` (exit 0)
  shows the branch is inside `master`; then `git merge --ff-only
  origin/master` and a plain `git push` restart it, no force needed.
